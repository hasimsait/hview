# Household objects and practical fittings

The catalogue adds 48 generic planning models. All names and descriptions are in English, and all geometry is generated locally for the application and portable editor.

Everyday Objects contains 31 models:

- Mirrors: arched wall mirror, full-length leaning mirror and tabletop vanity mirror.
- Crockery: dinner plate, six-plate stack, cereal bowl, serving bowl, mug and drinking glass.
- Countertop appliances: kettle, toaster, espresso machine, microwave, blender and air fryer.
- Laundry baskets: open basket, lidded hamper and two-compartment sorter.
- Bins: pedal bin, recycling sorter and open wastepaper basket.
- Coat hangers: wooden hanger, wire hanger and freestanding clothes rail with hangers.
- Desk equipment: monitor, laptop, keyboard, mouse, printer, letter trays and books.

Practical Details contains 17 models:

- Straight, bent and looped power cables, plus four- and six-socket extension leads with plugs.
- Straight waste pipe, 90-degree elbow, tee, P-trap and bottle trap.
- Washing-machine inlet and ribbed drain hoses, with visible connection collars.
- Small and large wall access panels.
- Floor-mounted, wall-mounted and wedge door stops.

## Placement and clearance checks

Widths, heights and depths are adjustable reference dimensions, including visible connectors. Cable and hose dimensions describe the route envelope, not the material length. Separate straight, bent, looped and hooked models provide common route shapes. Tube radii are constructed in metric space, so stretching a route in one direction does not flatten the cable cross-section. Very small envelopes scale down connectors to remain within the available space.

Practical fittings have position steps of 0.001 m and dimension steps of 0.1 cm. Use elevation and rotation to place them behind appliances or beneath cabinets. These models support visual clearance checks; they do not connect automatically to appliances, calculate flexible routes or detect collisions. Pipes and hoses have actual open bores, inner walls and annular connection faces. Ribbed hoses, basket lattices and keyboard keys merge repeated geometry to limit draw calls.

Surface objects start at reference table or counter heights; they do not automatically attach to the surface below. Access panels and wall stops use wall placement. Other practical fittings start on the floor or at a reference service height. All can be moved, resized, rotated, hidden or deleted using the existing inspector. Finishes can be applied independently to named sections such as Hatch, Frame, Hose, Coupling, Ceramic, Lid and Cover. Mirrors use the application's existing static mirror material.

Both canonical and legacy plan formats retain asset identity, dimensions, transforms, visibility and component finishes. Exported HTML includes the catalogue and local geometry without external model downloads.
